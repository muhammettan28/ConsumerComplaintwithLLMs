\subsection{QLoRA Adaptation with 500 Training Records}
\label{sec:qlora_results}

All three models and both modes completed every preserved test file.
Source/label alignment, original test and adapter hashes, raw-response
reparsing, paired batch sizes and recomputed pooled metrics were verified
for all 72,000 predictions. Table~\ref{tab:qlora_paired} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:qlora_paired} report the 500-character comparisons.
Balanced weighted F1 increases by 2.83 percentage points for Gemma,
7.92 for Qwen and 9.62 for Phi. The corresponding paired 95\,\% intervals
are [1.75, 3.96], [6.55, 9.34] and [8.21, 11.07] percentage points.
Qwen has the highest adapted balanced point estimate (0.7891), followed
by Phi (0.7845) and Gemma (0.7803). These point estimates do not establish
significant differences between adapted models.

All twelve paired F1 intervals exclude zero, including both length
variants and reduced imbalance. All twelve accuracy comparisons pass
Holm correction ($p_{\mathrm{adj}}\leq0.00142$).
For 250-character balanced inputs, adapted F1 is 0.7674, 0.7767 and
0.7794 for Gemma, Qwen and Phi; gains are 2.13, 7.17 and 9.70 percentage
points. Thus the gain is observed under both text limits, while their
paired records preclude treating them as independent replications.
Complete length and macro-F1 results appear in Supplementary
Table~\ref{S-tab:qlora_full}; class-specific scores appear in
Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:qlora_class}.

In reduced imbalance at 500 characters, adapted weighted F1 is
0.7948, 0.7946 and 0.7994, whereas macro-F1 is 0.7576, 0.7602 and
0.7643. The gap retains the importance of minority-class evaluation.
On balanced 500-character tests all five class F1 scores improve for each model;
Qwen's largest class gain is debt collection (16.68 percentage points),
and Phi's is retail banking (14.82 points). Across all test files,
none of the 36,000 adapted predictions is invalid. Base Gemma has two
invalid predictions, Qwen none and Phi 21. Improvements therefore extend
beyond output-format repair: balanced Gemma and Qwen bases already have
zero invalid predictions.

The training and serial timing results in Table~\ref{tab:qlora_resources}
fit the 16~GB GPU budget. Adaptation does not consistently accelerate
generation: Gemma and Qwen's measured serial means rise from 0.475 to
0.647~s and from 0.421 to 0.539~s, while Phi's falls from 0.487 to
0.435~s. These observations concern unmerged adapters and the measured
100-record sample, rather than a general serving-engine speedup.
Serial/batch prediction agreement is 100/99\,\% for base/adapted Gemma,
98/100\,\% for Qwen and 100/100\,\% for Phi. The three differences have
matching source hashes and input token counts; their computational cause
was not isolated. Full-test scores use the saved batched predictions.
Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:qlora_serial} gives timing and agreement details.
